Lots of browser games have leaderboards. Far fewer are fair enough to compete on. The difference usually isn't graphics or game design — it's what happens behind the score.

## The problem with random

Most arcade games are random by design. In a single-player game that's fun. In a competition it's a problem: if your rival got an easier board, a kinder pattern or a slower opening, their higher score doesn't prove they played better.

## The same seed for everyone

In RivalArcade every player in a contest plays **the identical game seed**. The bricks fall in the same rows, the fruit flies on the same arcs, the nodes appear in the same places at the same times. When you lose a 1v1, you lost on the same board your opponent played.

That's what turns twelve arcade games into competition formats:

- **1v1 duels:** you and one rival, one run each, higher score wins.
- **Live Arenas:** up to five players on one seed, top three paid.
- **Cups:** scheduled score-attack tournaments where the whole field plays the cup seed once.

Practice, by contrast, uses a random seed — useful for learning, not for proving anything.

## The server is the judge

Seeds only help if scores are honest. In RivalArcade, all scoring, rewards, purchases and ranks are computed on the server. The app never sends a balance, a reward or a result — only a run token and the score, which the server times and checks. Scores that are faster or higher than the game allows are rejected, and unusually large jumps are reviewed.

## One account per person

Every account verifies a mobile number, one account per number, which makes smurfing and multi-accounting much harder. Rewarded matches against the same opponent are also limited to three a day, so friends can't trade wins.

## Simple scoring you can audit

Every normal action in every game is worth one point, specials are labelled on screen, and the results breakdown adds up line by line. You can see exactly where a match was won.

## No pay-to-win

Rival Coins only buy cosmetics. Nothing in the store changes hitboxes, speed or scoring.

Fair competition isn't a feature you bolt on later. It's the foundation — and it's why RivalArcade is built the way it is.
